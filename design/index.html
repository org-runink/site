Where a claim stands

Runink publishes where every claim stands instead of publishing a number. That habit is the identity. One shape carries it, and the shape works in greyscale, at eleven pixels, and on a photocopy.

Runs

Solid: a record. Something that exists and was observed. Also first-party.

Hypothetical

Hollow: a description. Something stated rather than measured. Also drawn, self-declared, not measured.

The rule about colour

Colour has two jobs

Signal — one orange, and it means a person can act here. Links, focus, the primary action, the approval seam. It is never used for emphasis or decoration, so an orange thing on a Runink page is always something you can do.

Category — which industry or product this is. A category colour is never legible on its own; the industry is always named in text beside it, which is why two of the five can sit close in hue without costing anything.

Nothing else. Standing is fill. Emphasis is weight. Hierarchy is scale and rule. In particular, standing is deliberately not colour: hue would make runs look good and hypothetical look bad, and they are not better and worse — they are different kinds of statement.

Signal

Runink's orange, held at two values that measure. The palette that this one replaces published #ca4708 as usable: it is 4.76:1 on white, but only 4.22:1 on the whitepaper's own paper (#F5F1E8) and on primary.50 (#eef1fc) — under AA on the two grounds it most often sat on.

Category

Five industries, two bindings each. Every value measures AA or better on all three grounds of its mode, so the raw accent is legible as text and needs no darkening step. Four are the same hue family as the accent the section already used, corrected to a usable value. Logistics is the one that genuinely moves: it was #ea580c — the signal hue — which made "this is a category" and "you can click this" the same colour on that page. Type

One superfamily, three jobs

Fira, self-hosted under the SIL Open Font License. A superfamily is the typographic form of the product's own claim: faces drawn to different briefs, made to read as one voice. Every face on this site is served from its own hardware, and no page fetches a typeface from a third party — a company arguing that your material should not leave your hardware should not be sending a request to Google on every page load to render its own name.

Aa

Fira Sans Condensed — Heavy 800, SemiBold 600

Display and headings. Narrow and dense, with the weight of a title block on a technical drawing rather than the roundness of a product hero.

Radius

Nothing above 12px. The system this replaces went to 40px, which is the radius of a consumer app card and belongs to a different argument. This material is a document, and a document has a corner. The standing mark is at 0 because a square that must read as filled-or-hollow at 7px cannot afford a curve.

Two shadows, for things that genuinely float — a menu, a dialog. Everything else expresses depth with a rule and a change of ground, because that is the part that survives being printed. The previous system's neon glows are gone: a glow means nothing on paper.

The mark

The drawing holds. The file did not.

A herding dog in profile: it reads a field, gathers what is scattered into one picture, and brings it to a named handler who decides. Nothing about that needs redrawing. What needed fixing was the artefact — a 512px raster with a cream background baked in, which is why the site header has always had to crop it into a tile. It is now one vector path set to currentColor, so it takes the ink of whatever ground it sits on, at any size, on paper.
