{{- /* The page's own JSON-LD node. partials/schema-org.html carries the Organization, the WebSite and the breadcrumbs; this says what THIS page is. WHAT CHANGED, AND WHY (2026-10). - Every regular page used to be typed `Article`: the product pages, the pricing pages, the company page, the privacy policy. An Article asks a consumer to look for an author and a publication date, and Google's Article guidance is for news, blog and sports articles. Now only pages under /blog/ (the posts and the papers) are Articles; every other page is a `WebPage`, and an index is a `CollectionPage`. - The home page emitted a SECOND WebSite node, with no @id, beside the one schema-org.html already emits with one, plus an empty `"about": []`. Two WebSite entities for one site is a contradiction a parser has to resolve; the home page now relies on the canonical node. - The `case_studies` → citation and `compliance` → about branches are gone. No page has ever set either key. `compliance` was the same trap trust-signals.html already removed from the visible page: one line of front matter would have published a certification claim in machine-readable form, and Runink holds no certification. `partnerships` → mentions stays, because naming a company we actually work with is what CONTENT.md rule 11 permits. - A product page now names its product as a SoftwareApplication (partials/schema-software.html) and points its WebPage's mainEntity at it, so an answer engine can resolve "Runink TIDE" to a product made by the Organization rather than to a web page about something. The JSON is built as a dict and serialised with jsonify, not hand-written with commas between `with` blocks: the hand-written form is valid only for as long as every optional key happens to be present or absent in the right combination. */ -}} {{- $org := dict "@id" "https://runink.org/#organization" -}} {{- $site := dict "@id" "https://runink.org/#website" -}} {{- $isArticle := and .IsPage (eq .Section "blog") -}} {{- $software := partial "schema-software.html" . -}} {{- /* The page's image: the same choice seo-head.html makes for og:image, so the two never disagree.
