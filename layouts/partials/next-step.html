WHY IT EXISTS. /library/, /blog/, /blog/whitepapers/ and /use-cases/ are the four pages a reader reaches by choosing to go deeper, and every one of them ended by sending the reader sideways into more reading. /use-cases/ was the sharpest version: it closes with "Bring one lane, one carrier, or one month of returns. A short conversation is usually enough" — an invitation with no door on it. The highest-intent pages on the site had the thinnest exit. WHY IT DOES NOT POINT AT /#contact-form. That link is how 429 of 433 pages convert, and it costs the reader a full page load, a scroll past the whole homepage, and the context they arrived with. They then meet a box asking what problem they are trying to solve — a question they had already answered by choosing this page. So the link carries ?about=, and partials/home/contact.html puts that value into the message box as editable text the reader can see and delete. VISIBLE, NOT HIDDEN. The obvious way to do this is a hidden form field, and it is the wrong way. The form says, under its own submit button, "We use what you send here to reply to you and for nothing else." A covert field contradicts a promise printed on the page, on a site whose argument is that you should not have to take a claim on trust. Rule 10 says never publish the mechanics of how the site treats the reader; the answer to that is not to have mechanics worth hiding. What is carried is a short phrase in the reader's own language, in a box they are looking at. `about` is front matter per page and per language, so it is a label somebody wrote rather than a URL scraped at runtime.
