Logistics & Supply Chain

The evidence of your next loss is already in your systems.

An entry held for a missing paper while the port charge runs. A refrigerated load drifting warm overnight. A claim still inside its filing window. All recorded somewhere already, and nobody with the hours to assemble it. This page is Runink FACE, the product this company is built around.

Where it goes wrong

  • The hold nobody joined to the cost

    An entry sits at the port for a missing paper. The notice arrives with everything else from overnight, and the daily charge runs until somebody joins it to the invoice.

  • Claims that expire quietly

    One claim means the carrier's receipt, the weighbridge reading, the rate on the date, the deadline and a drafted letter. Only the largest are worth the morning.

  • Temperature you read after the write-off

    A container drifts out of range overnight and the reading is already in the sensor data. The window between saving the load and writing it off is hours.

  • Demand that grew on the way up the chain

    Every stage rounds up to a full case and adds a safety margin, so the plant builds for demand that never existed. The amplification lives in the sequence, across four systems.

Who owns this

The people whose week it changes.

  • Operations director

    You find out before your customer does, and the fix is drafted when you do.

  • Chief financial officer

    Recoveries stop being a heroic effort and become a pipeline you can read.

  • Trade compliance

    A held entry arrives with the reason, the papers that release it and the charge accruing.

  • Head of planning

    Safety margins are argued from your own numbers, not by seniority.

  • Chief information officer

    A shorter security review, because the data stays on your machines.

What Runink FACE changes

  • A new instance shows you an empty queue. FACE arrives holding no findings about your operation and does not manufacture any — what appears is what it read once you connected it to something.
  • What does appear is a specific proposed action with the rule it invoked and the records it cited attached, in one queue, rather than a dashboard for somebody to interpret.
  • A named person approves, edits or rejects each one, and the decision is written down as an event carrying who decided and what they changed.
  • Approving is what sends it — and the reply names the parts that did not go. No mail connector configured, no write-back into your ERP: each is returned as a named skipped step with its reason, on every reply, including one where half the work went out. Write-back into an ERP is not built today, and it is worth asking us which of your systems FACE can write to before planning around it.
  • Where a check could not run, the answer is that it could not run — written out as not a finding that the thing is compliant. A quantity nobody measured is kept as unmeasured with a reason rather than rounded to zero, and a connection nobody has contacted is never reported as verified.
  • Later, why a claim was filed or an entry held is answered from the record.

Customs hold · A container held at the port, from hold to cleared

  1. The hold shows up

    An entry comes back held, under examination or detained, and the count of days it has been held is above zero. That combination is the whole test — it is a fixed rule, not a judgement, and it runs against every entry rather than the ones somebody thought to check.

  2. The cost is counted from your own rate

    The days it has been held, multiplied by the per-day demurrage rate in your own agreement. That is the arithmetic in full. It is the figure that is already accruing while the entry sits in a queue nobody reads end to end.

  3. The missing paper is named

    The reason for the hold and the documents outstanding against it come off the entry record and are stated on the item, so the person who picks it up is not starting by finding out what is wrong.

  4. The accountable party is checked

    Separately, entries are read for an importer of record that is blank, or filled in with the consignee, or with a placeholder somebody typed once. Those carry duty and tax with nobody accountable for them, and they are raised as their own item with the amount at stake attached.

  5. Every pound is counted once

    Demurrage on a held entry and duty on an unattributed one are different money, and counting them as one number is the commonest way this sort of total gets inflated. They stay separate, deliberately, and there is a test that fails if they ever merge.

  6. A named person decides

    The item waits. Approving it is what sends anything, and who approved it, when, and what they changed is written down. If part of what was drafted could not be carried out, the result names that part rather than reporting success.

Cold chain · A reefer that drifted warm, from the door to a filed claim

  1. The frame arrives

    A photograph taken on a handheld at the door, or a still pulled off a yard camera. Before anything reads it, it is checked to be an image: the header is decoded on its own, the format has to be one of two, and the size is capped both in bytes and in pixels. A PDF, a video container or raw bytes are refused at that step.

  2. A model reads it, on your hardware

    The frame is reduced to a size the model can take and read by a vision model running on machines you control. What comes back is a written observation tied to the exact frame it was read from, so the sentence and the evidence for it stay together.

  3. The paperwork is read beside it

    The consignment record, the handover, and the condition the documents say the load should be in. The observation is set against what was already written down rather than against a threshold somebody picked.

  4. What differs comes out named

    The pallet, the crate, the container door — named, in the words a person would use. A severity score cannot be argued with in front of a carrier. A named part of a named consignment can.

  5. The call reaches the person who owns it

    A cue can be broadcast to whatever is subscribed to the yard's event stream, and the record keeps it as what it is: requested. What happens next is somebody's decision, and the record says so rather than implying a movement was stopped.

  6. A named person decides

    The item waits, exactly as the held entry does. Approving it is what sends anything, and who approved it, when, and what they changed is written down.

Customs filing · A manifest that came back rejected, from the filing to the paper it named

  1. The filing is read, not the clearance

    An entry can be stopped at the border on a manifest the authority accepted, and a filing can be rejected while the box is still moving. Held is a clearance state and rejected is a filing state. Each is read where it is written, and neither is ever read as the other.

  2. The code travels as it was written

    The exception code the rejection carried comes across word for word, with the manifest it belongs to, the port it was filed at and who filed it. Your broker gets the reference to quote back rather than somebody's paraphrase of it.

  3. Each missing paper is named on its own line

    Every document the filing named as outstanding comes out as its own item, carrying the record it was read from. What is waiting on whom is the first thing on the page rather than the thing somebody works out from it.

  4. Silence is written down as silence

    Where the records do not say what the filing came back as, that is raised as its own finding. Reading the clearance status as the filing status would assert a filing nobody declared, so it is never done — an entry can sit on a hold with its manifest accepted.

  5. The delay is counted once

    Demurrage running on that entry is already counted by the held-entry item above, so here it travels as what is at stake rather than as a second charge. One delay counted twice is the commonest way a total gets inflated, and there is a test that fails if the two ever merge.

  6. A named person decides

    What leaves is the document a broker or a port authority is shown, and it leaves when somebody approves it. Who approved it, when, and what they changed stays with it.

Equipment limits · A machine drifting towards the limit its own feed states

  1. A limit nobody stated is not a limit

    The figure a reading is compared against is read out of the feed itself — an alarm temperature, a trip point, a replace-below level. Where the feed states only what the machine ran at when it was healthy, the drift is reported and nothing is projected against it, because a healthy baseline is not a point at which anything fails.

  2. The readings are compared in order

    The first reading in the window, the latest one, how many were comparable, and how far the latest sits from the stated limit. Each figure carries the reading it came from, so your engineer checks the arithmetic against the feed instead of taking it on trust.

  3. The line drawn through them is arithmetic, not a forecast

    Where the readings support it, a straight line through the first and the last says when it would reach the stated limit at that rate. That is a description of two readings, and every document of this kind says so on its face.

  4. No probability, no confidence, no remaining life

    None of the three is on the document, and that is structural rather than a matter of wording: there is no field for them to go in. A failure score fitted to a handful of readings reads as a measurement and is not one. A number a planner can recompute is worth more than a confident one they cannot.

  5. A named person schedules the work

    The item reaches maintenance with the machine, the channel, the stated limit and the readings behind it. Approving is what raises the job, and who approved it, when, and what they changed stays on the record.

The jobs this covers

Each one has a page of its own: what it reads, what it hands to a person, and how you would know it worked.

  • Reverse logistics — returns, and what to do with them

    Where a return goes — shelf, refurbishment, recycling or disposal — drafted at the scan, from a policy written down so that the same grade gets the same answer every time.

  • Cold chain, and what the yard camera already sees

    A frame from a yard or infrared camera, checked to be an image and read by a vision model on hardware you control, against the handover record and the paperwork the load travels with.

  • Route planning that keeps up with the day

    The plan that was right at six in the morning is not right by ten, and nobody re-runs it because re-running it means planning a day by hand. This is about making the second run cost nothing.

  • Stock cover and supplier planning

    Seeing a stockout while there is still time to order normally, rather than after the safety stock has gone and the difference is being paid in air freight.

  • Demand forecasting

    A line starts moving weeks before the reorder point does. Reading that turn out of your own order history while it is still a forecasting problem.

  • Freight claims and port charges

    The entry, the hold, the reason, the documents missing from the file, the days held and the per-day charge, gathered and joined, so a reviewer is left judging whether the case holds.

  • Seeing the whole chain, not your end of it

    Each supplier, carrier and warehouse holds one piece of the picture, in its own format. The picture itself is not held anywhere, and assembling it is the morning nobody has.

  • Hands-free dispatch, for drivers without a screen

    The person who knows a load is late is driving. Asking and answering out loud puts the fact on the record while the truck keeps moving, and the desk still makes the change.

  • Testing a plan before you commit to it

    A port shuts and you have a day to pick a new route. State the change and the rules it touches — reorder points, lead times, service commitments — and read back which rules it collides with, in what order.

  • Customer data privacy and emissions reporting

    Personal details stripped out of log and diagnostic output before it is written, and a lane's emissions worked out from one published road factor and the distance, with the method written on the figure.

Two things that make the above possible

  • Your data stays on your machines

    The order files, the customs papers, the readings and the reasoning about them stay on hardware you control, and the model FACE reasons with is one you run: no third-party model dependency, and exactly one inference endpoint — the one you point it at. Two paths do deliberately reach outside, because they must: a route request goes to the routing service you configure, and open-web research puts a query to a public search endpoint. Neither carries your records.

    What you would notice
    The security review a shipper runs before it will let you hold its order data, and the customer-data clauses that today need an exception. With no model provider in the path, both have less to argue with. Ask us to walk the boundary rather than taking the sentence: this is how the software is built, not something a test enforces, so it is a code review you can run and not a certificate we hold.

  • Open-web research with no account attached to it

    When an answer needs the open web — a carrier's standing, a customs ruling, a published rate, a party you are unsure about shipping to — the search runs from your own infrastructure through a public search endpoint, and your own browser then fetches and reads the pages behind the results.

    What you would notice
    Not secrecy from the search engine, which sees the query as it would from any browser. What you get is the absence of an account: no API key, no vendor contract, no per-question bill, so no supplier is accumulating a searchable history of the consignees your company has been asking about, filed under your company's name. It also means how much checking a shipment gets is decided by the shipment rather than by a monthly query cap.
