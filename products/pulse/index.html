What is Runink PULSE?

Runink PULSE is marketing software from Runink for small and mid-size businesses and the people who sell and market for them. It finds the companies worth calling, drafts the reply, the campaign and the follow-up, and shows which channel brings customers in, while your team decides what goes out. It runs on Runink's shared machines, in your own cloud account or inside your own estate, and it is priced per seat, sold on its own.

Who PULSE is for.

The owner of a small or mid-size business, and the people who sell and market for it. You know the week. Messages from new leads sit until somebody has a minute. Campaign copy gets written at night, after the real work. An agency retainer pays for routine posts. Several tools each charge per seat, and it is still hard to say which channel brought a customer in.

PULSE puts that work in one place. It is a separate product from FACE and from Runink TIDE, sold on its own.

Four jobs that eat your week, done from one brief.

Each stage feeds the next from the same reading of your business. Nobody writes a fresh brief for every tool. What PULSE drafts goes to a review queue, and your team decides which channels it may go out on.

1. Diagnose

You pay for a website. Is it bringing anyone in?

Most owners cannot answer that without paying someone to look. PULSE looks for you. Give it your web address. It scores your site on nine measures, writes a plain diagnosis, and ranks what to fix first. You act on the list from the same screen.

  • Site Audit Each measure is a set of checks you can read. Can search engines read your pages? How fast do they load? Who links to you? Is there something to click and a way to buy? A check that needs a source you have not connected is set aside and listed by name, and the rest of the measure is weighed around it. Something nobody could measure does not lower your score — and each measure says how much of it was measured, so a high score on thin coverage cannot pass for a high score on full coverage. The weightings behind all nine are settings you can read, so you can disagree with one in particular rather than with the score in general.
  • One line to start Type "Audit" and your web address into the assistant, or say it. PULSE reads your site, scores it and writes the diagnosis. Then it fills in the rest from that one reading: drafts waiting for your approval, a draft campaign, a 30-day follow-up plan, customer profiles, a draft course, market research and the first leads. It runs in minutes, and it keeps running if you close the page. Ask again within a day and you get that same audit back, with the date it ran on it, until you say run it again.
  • Every step says where it got that The audit comes back as a list of steps. A step that wrote something says whether it was read on the web, worked out by the model, or both, and shows the first address it read. A step that could not finish says why, in words, and the page it would have filled stays empty. Nothing is filled with something made up to look complete.
  • Your channels, read together Your website, LinkedIn, Instagram and TikTok, turned into one written business analysis. Export it and put it in front of your partners or your board.
  • What it is worth Opex. Fewer outside audits bought from an agency or consultant. Fewer hours spent guessing which fix matters most.

2. Attract

Leads go cold while you are busy.

A message lands on WhatsApp during a customer visit. A list of prospects waits for someone to write the first email. By the time anyone gets to it, the lead has talked to somebody else. PULSE does the first part of that work, so your people spend their time on the conversations.

  • Finding the companies Describe the kind of customer you want. PULSE searches the public web for companies that match. For each one it drafts a cold email, a call script and a LinkedIn message, written from what it read about that company. Leads move through a pipeline: new, contacted, qualified, won or lost. They sync with HubSpot, so sales keeps working where it already works.
  • Knowing the market Topics trending in your sector, competitor channels worth watching, and the podcasts, publications and events where your subject belongs. What an audit finds stays on the Market view, each item with the site it was found on and the date. Shows worth a guest spot also join your influencer list, scored by how well they fit your subject. An audience size appears only where the source actually stated one. Mark a result useful or not, and the next round learns from it.
  • Answering on WhatsApp Connect WhatsApp through your own Twilio account, and PULSE can answer messages from people already in your HubSpot contacts, at any hour. Each incoming message is treated as information, never as an instruction, and each reply is checked before it is sent. Once WhatsApp is connected, PULSE answers on its own, and one setting turns those replies off.
  • Phone calls PULSE places a call to a lead through a phone exchange you host yourself, so there is no per-minute calling service to pay. The sales agent hears what the person says and answers in its own voice, while the call is happening. Outbound calling starts switched off: an administrator turns it on, and only people with editing rights place calls. Consent, calling hours and recording notices stay your decision, as the law requires.
  • What it is worth Opex. Fewer sales hours spent building lists and writing first drafts. No per-minute calling bill. And revenue that leaks today, every time a lead waits too long for an answer.
3. Create

Campaign copy, written at night, after the real work.

Or paid for every month, through an agency retainer for posts that are mostly routine. PULSE drafts that work for you. One brief becomes LinkedIn posts, blog articles, whitepapers and a course. Studio adds a two-host podcast script, a hook-first script with its shot list for a short video, a deck written slide by slide with speaker notes, an infographic laid out section by section, and a report over the period you name. Every piece starts from the same reading of your business, so the message holds across channels.

The text appears on screen as it is written, so a wrong angle is caught in the second paragraph, not on page nine. A longer piece says which part of the work it is on — waiting for the model, writing, then the editorial pass — and a piece that was cut short says so. Half a document is never handed to you as a finished one.

  • Every draft has a status Each piece carries one status: draft, waiting for review, approved, rejected, published, archived. At any moment you see what is waiting on you and what actually went out.
  • A LinkedIn post every day For each active campaign, PULSE drafts one LinkedIn post a day and puts it in your review queue. Copy it with one click and post it from your own profile.
  • A plan for the month PULSE writes a channel-by-channel plan, then turns it into a dated schedule: what publishes, on which channel, in which week. It queues only on the channels you have switched on.
  • What it is worth Opex. Agency hours for routine copy. Evenings an owner spends writing. A separate scheduler and a separate writing tool, each with its own seat price.
4. Retain

Which channel is bringing customers in? And are they staying?

Winning a customer gets the attention. Keeping them pays the bills. Most small teams learn a customer has gone quiet when the renewal does not come. PULSE keeps the follow-up on a schedule, and keeps the numbers next to it.

  • Channel results, in plain words Impressions, clicks, conversions and return, day by day and split by channel, read from the accounts you connect. A written note says what the numbers mean, and a forecast carries a range around it so you can see how much of it is guesswork. Content Gaps compares what you publish with your competitors, and names the questions your market asks that you have not answered.
  • A figure it could not get stays blank PULSE shows only figures it actually read. A number it could not obtain is shown as a dash. It is never rounded down to zero and never estimated, so a quiet channel and a channel nobody measured never look the same on the screen.
  • One profile per customer What a customer or segment cares about, what they respond to, and where they stand with you. It lists the touches that account has had, and suggests the one that should come next.
  • 30-day follow-up A weekly planner, tasks you move along, saved check-ins, and a score with its history. A cooling customer shows up as a trend, not a surprise at renewal.
  • What it is worth Opex. Ad spend kept on channels that convert, and taken off the ones that do not. Revenue kept, because a quiet customer gets a call in time.

Your team decides what goes out.

PULSE drafts, and your team decides what goes out. Every draft, whether a post, a cold email, a call script or a whitepaper, arrives with a status and an approve and a reject. The switch that decides whether anything leaves on a channel is separate, and it starts off. WhatsApp replies are the exception: once WhatsApp is connected, PULSE answers on its own until the owner turns that setting off.

Channels start switched off

One main switch, then one per channel, decides what may publish. A channel that is off sends nothing, even an approved draft. Approving a draft says the content is right. Switching a channel on says it may go out. On the channels that post as the company itself — its own LinkedIn, its blog, its podcast — only the people the owner names by name can publish at all. Material held back is marked as held back, with the reason, so you can tell it apart from material that published and material that failed.

A second pass before it reaches you

The agents propose. Before a prescription, a content gap or a plan for the quarter is saved, a judge checks it against the evidence the run actually gathered. One the judge disagrees with is held back and listed as held back. One it could not rule on is kept and marked as such. Disagreement is never shown to you as agreement, and a held-back prescription never becomes a draft or a scheduled post.

No outside AI service sees your lists

Your positioning, your customer lists and your pipeline are processed on the machines your plan names: your own servers or cloud account on Dedicated and Enterprise, Runink's shared machines on Lite. Runink runs its own model there. No outside company runs a database holding your working data, and none is sent a prompt about it.

One seat price, not a stack of subscriptions.

A scheduler, a writing tool, a lead list and an agency retainer each send their own invoice. PULSE is priced per seat, and the seat covers the work that person puts through it. Writing a second version of a campaign does not add to the price, so it gets tried.

It runs where you put it

The same software runs on one workstation to try it out, and on one machine or several inside your own estate for daily use. PULSE Lite runs on Runink's shared machines instead. No outside AI service is called on any plan. The console opens in a browser, on an Android phone and on a Linux desktop, in English, Spanish, French and Portuguese.

No graphics card to buy

PULSE runs on ordinary processors. A trial needs a well-specified developer workstation, not a server purchase. That is capital you keep.

Volume does not change the price

A team that writes ten pieces a month and a team that writes two hundred pay for the same seats. The price stays the same. On Lite, each seat gets 10 tasks a day at full speed; after that, tasks keep running at a slower pace until midnight in your time zone.

The questions that actually get asked.

Straight answers to what buyers ask first.

Is PULSE the same thing as Runink FACE?
No. They are separate products. PULSE is for marketing and sales: site and social audit, market research, finding leads and the material a marketing team publishes. FACE reads logistics, fulfilment and claims records and drafts an action for a named person to approve. Nothing on this page is a FACE capability. Runink TIDE is a third product, also sold separately: the operations layer for your engineering and data teams, on your servers, your cloud or ours. Its paper describes it.
How do I answer new leads faster?
Take the first part of the work off your people. Describe the customer you want, and PULSE finds companies that match and drafts a cold email, a call script and a LinkedIn message for each. Connect WhatsApp through your own Twilio account, and it can answer people already in your HubSpot contacts at any hour, with each reply checked before it is sent. Once WhatsApp is connected it answers on its own, and one setting turns those replies off.
How do I spend less on an agency for routine posts?
Draft the routine work yourselves, from one brief. PULSE turns one brief into LinkedIn posts, blog articles, whitepapers and a course, and Studio adds a two-host podcast script, a short-video script with its shot list, a deck with speaker notes, an infographic laid out section by section, and a report over the period you name. It also writes a dated plan of what publishes on which channel in which week. Every draft carries a review status, and nothing goes out on a channel until someone switches that channel on. The seat price stays the same however many pieces you write.
Can we run PULSE on our own servers?
Yes. The same software runs on one workstation to try it out, and on one machine or several inside your own estate for daily use. It runs on ordinary processors, with no graphics card required, and no outside AI service is called. PULSE Lite runs on Runink’s shared machines instead; the cost answer below lists each plan and where it runs.
Does PULSE publish on its own, or do we?
You decide. Every draft — a post, a cold email, a call script, a whitepaper — carries a status and an approve or reject. The control that decides whether anything leaves is separate. Every publishing channel stays off until someone switches it on, with one main switch and one per channel. A channel that is off sends nothing, whatever status a draft carries, and switching a channel on is the decision that lets material go out on it. On the channels that post as the company itself — its own LinkedIn, its blog, its podcast — only the people the owner names by name can publish at all. WhatsApp replies go out on their own once WhatsApp is connected, only to people already in your HubSpot contacts, and the owner can turn them off with one setting.
Does it need training on our business first?
No. There is nothing to label or upload in advance. When PULSE writes, it reads your own material at the moment of the question — your site audit, your positioning documents, your earlier work and your customer records, held on the machines your plan names — and keeps the source next to the draft. Delete a document and PULSE stops using it. Nothing about your business is baked into the model, which is the same file on your first day and your five hundredth.
What happens when it cannot work something out?
It says so. If a Site Audit check depends on a source you have not connected, that check is set aside and named, and the rest of the measure is weighed around it. The score is not quietly lowered by something nobody measured. In the one-line audit, each step records where its result came from — read on the web, worked out by the model, or both — and a step that could not finish gives the reason, while the page it would have filled stays empty. A source that failed is reported as failed, separate from one that did not apply. And a channel that is switched off stays off, even for approved material. That material is marked as held back, with a stated reason, so you can tell it apart from both published and failed.
How do we know it is not making the numbers up?
Because it only shows figures it read. Impressions, clicks, conversions and return come from the advertising and analytics accounts you connect, and nowhere else — an audit never writes a performance number. A figure PULSE could not obtain is shown as a dash rather than a zero or an estimate. An influencer or podcast found by research carries a score for how well it fits your subject, not an audience figure, and an audience size appears only where the source stated one. Every audit score opens out into the measures beneath it and then into the individual checks, so a number is always traceable to what was examined.
How does it work with the systems we already run?
PULSE connects to the accounts you already have — your social accounts, Google Ads and Google Analytics, and HubSpot. You create the token or key on the platform itself, paste it into the console, and test the connection there before you rely on it; it is stored sealed, never shown again, and you disconnect it from the same screen. Leads sync into HubSpot rather than PULSE trying to become your customer-record system, so sales keeps working where it already works.
Where does our data go?
Onto the machines your plan names: your own servers or cloud account on Dedicated and Enterprise, Runink’s shared machines on Lite. Working data is kept in a database inside the application itself, copied continuously into file storage on those same machines, and restored from that copy when the application starts. No outside company runs a database holding your positioning, your customer records or your pipeline. No material is sent out to be trained on, and there is no account with an outside AI provider for it to be sent to.
Does it need special hardware?
No. PULSE runs on ordinary processors, with no graphics card required. A trial wants a well-specified developer workstation — a reasonable amount of memory, a decent number of processor cores and free storage — rather than a server purchase.
Is there anything we can put in someone's hands today?
An Android build of the console is on the downloads page. You install it directly from that page.
What does it cost?
PULSE is priced by the seat: you pay for each person who uses it, and the seat covers the work that person puts through it. PULSE Lite runs on Runink’s shared machines, where your work takes its turn: $59 a seat a month, or $49 a seat a month on an annual plan, for teams of one to nine. Each Lite seat gets 10 tasks a day at full speed; after that, tasks keep running at a slower pace until midnight in your time zone, when the count starts again. Dedicated is $99 a seat a month, for ten seats and up, running on runners in your own cloud account, which bills you for that compute directly. Enterprise, including deployments with no connection to the outside world, is quoted with you. PULSE, FACE and TIDE are each sold on their own subscription, so a PULSE seat is a seat in PULSE.
