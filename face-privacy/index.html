FACE — Privacy Policy

This policy covers the FACE mobile app published by Runink (Android application id org.runink.face) and the FACE deployment it connects to. FACE is a business console. The general privacy policy covers your organisation’s account, its billing, and the operational data FACE processes for it. This page covers what the app itself adds: sign-in, the camera, the microphone, and what stays on the phone.

Who can use FACE

FACE is not open to the public. Neither the app nor the deployment has a sign-up form, and you cannot register yourself.

  • An administrator of your organisation’s FACE instance adds you on the instance’s Admin page. That page saves the grant to the instance’s member list.
  • Google sign-in works only for addresses on that list. A Google account that is not on it is refused. If the instance has Google sign-in configured but no list, nobody can sign in with Google. The first time a listed address signs in, FACE creates an account for it with the role the administrator granted.
  • Email and password sign-in is mainly for the instance’s operator-managed administrator account. An instance can switch password sign-in off for everyone else.
  • When an administrator removes you, FACE deletes your account record and ends your open sessions. Until the platform refreshes the instance’s sign-in list and FACE restarts, a removed address can still sign in with Google, and that sign-in creates a new read-only account. The Admin page says so when it happens.

What FACE collects

Your account

  • Email address. With Google sign-in, the app asks Google for your basic profile (openid, email, profile). It sends only Google’s signed identity token to your FACE deployment. FACE accepts the token only when Google reports the email as verified. We never see your Google password.
  • Your account record holds: an internal id, your email address, a hash of your password (bcrypt), your two-factor secret if you set one up, your role, and the date the account was created. It holds no name. A Google-created account gets a random password that nobody knows.
  • Your session records who you are (id, email, role), your organisation’s tenant, and how you signed in. It expires 12 hours after sign-in. Signing out ends every session of yours on the server first. Only then is the token removed from the phone.
  • Server logs record sign-in events, including the email address that signed in or was refused.

Camera

FACE uses the camera on one screen, and only while that screen is open.

  • Live detection. While the camera screen is open, the app keeps sending single frames to your FACE deployment so it can draw boxes, labels and any QR codes or barcodes (QR, Code 128, EAN/UPC, DataMatrix) over the preview. The deployment’s own detector and its own code reader handle these frames, with no AI model involved. These frames are not kept. They write no record, no history entry and no stored image. What you see on screen is the only output.
  • Photos you take. A photo you take with the shutter is attached to your question in the app. It is sent to the deployment for analysis only when you press Grade. Grading sends that one frame to the deployment’s vision model. FACE stores the result, not the photo: what was recognised, its material, condition grade and damage estimate, the time, and the camera label. We call this a “sighting”, and FACE reads back 30 days of sightings.
  • Annotated evidence images. Each FACE instance can choose to keep an annotated copy of a graded frame (the picture with FACE’s boxes drawn on it). This is off unless the instance’s operator turns it on. When it is on:
    • A frame is kept only if grading found an object to inspect. A frame whose only content is people is not kept, and neither is a frame where nothing was found.
    • FACE serves the image for at most 30 days.
    • Any signed-in member of the same organisation can open it, whatever their role.
    • The un-annotated original is never kept.
    • To be precise about the 30 days: they are a limit on showing the image, not a deletion. FACE’s storage layer cannot delete what it wrote. The encrypted file stays in storage until the operator removes it with a storage expiry rule.
  • Video clips recorded in the assistant panel are attached to your question as a file. The deployment samples frames from the clip and reads them the way it reads a photo. The clip is written to a temporary file for sampling, and that file is deleted as soon as sampling ends. What FACE keeps is the text it read from the clip (see “Files you attach”).
  • Location metadata. FACE does not ask for location permission. Photos taken inside the app therefore carry no GPS position. The app does not strip other metadata before sending.

Files you attach

  • Files you pick from the phone are sent to the deployment byte for byte, including any metadata they carry (for example, the GPS tags in a photo taken by another camera app).
  • The deployment reads each file in memory: text, tables and form fields. It stores what it read, up to 64 KB of text per file, with the run that used it, so that the run’s history can show it later. It does not store the file itself.
  • Site plans are different: a site plan you upload on the Twins map is stored as the original file.

Microphone and voice

FACE uses the microphone only when you tap a microphone button. There is no background listening and no wake word.

  • The command bar and the Fetch Center microphone. These record your voice command and send the audio to your FACE deployment, where FACE’s own speech model turns it into text. The audio is not stored. FACE’s voice audit log records sizes, never words: how many bytes were heard, how many characters were spoken, which command it was, and whether it succeeded. The text then becomes your question, and a question is kept the way a typed one is.
  • The assistant panel’s microphone uses the phone’s own speech recogniser, not FACE’s model. The command bar also falls back to the phone’s recogniser when the deployment’s speech service cannot be reached. In both cases your voice goes to whatever recogniser the phone provides, and only the resulting text reaches FACE. On most Android phones that recogniser is Google’s, and Google’s terms govern it, not ours.
  • Spoken replies. FACE’s own text-to-speech engine produces them on the deployment. It keeps the generated audio on the deployment’s disk, filed under a hash of the reply text, so that a repeated reply plays at once. That audio cache is not encrypted and has no expiry. When the deployment’s voice cannot be reached, the phone’s own text-to-speech engine reads the reply, so the reply text goes to that engine.

On your phone

  • Your session token is stored in Android’s encrypted secure storage (the Keystore-backed flutter_secure_storage), together with your organisation’s subscription tier. Signing out removes both.
  • The app keeps a local cache of recent fetch runs, traces, connection summaries and runners, so that history opens offline. It also keeps a queue of changes waiting to reach the server. This cache is an ordinary, unencrypted database in the app’s private storage. Signing out clears it, after your sessions end on the server; only the app’s language setting is kept. A change that had not reached the server yet is dropped at sign-out rather than sent later under whoever signs in next. The cache is excluded from Android backup and device transfer, so it never leaves the phone.

What FACE does not collect

  • No advertising, no analytics and no crash-reporting SDK. The app contains no Firebase, Crashlytics, Sentry or any analytics or attribution library.
  • No location. The app does not request location permission. The Twins map loads its map tiles only from your FACE deployment.
  • No contacts, calendar, SMS, call log, photo library access, or installed-app list. The app’s Android permissions, as built, are: internet access, network state, camera, microphone, and audio settings. Files reach FACE only through the system file picker, when you choose them.

Where your data goes

The app talks to one server: your organisation’s FACE deployment. Its AI models (language, vision and speech) run on machines Runink operates, or on your organisation’s own machines where its plan says so. FACE has no code path that sends your camera frames, photos, voice or questions to an outside AI provider.

Third parties the app itself contacts

  • Google, for sign-in, through Google Play services on the phone. This happens only when you choose Google sign-in.
  • Your phone’s own speech recogniser and text-to-speech engine, only in the cases described under “Microphone and voice”.

Third parties your deployment may contact for your organisation

These are server-side features of FACE, not of the app. The general policy covers them:

  • Public web search. When an analysis searches the public web, the deployment sends search queries to public search engines (DuckDuckGo, Bing, Brave, Mojeek, Startpage) and reads the pages they return.
  • Messaging carrier. If your organisation configures one, a result you choose to send by WhatsApp, SMS or voice call goes through that carrier. The app shows you a preview and asks you to confirm each send.

We do not sell your data, and we do not share it with anyone for advertising.

Data FACE processes for your organisation

FACE connects to your organisation’s own systems, such as ERPs, files, cameras and sensors, using credentials your administrators enter. It reads from them during fetch runs and keeps the results, run history and decisions for your organisation. That processing, and your rights over it, are covered by the general privacy policy. Connection credentials and FACE’s account and application tables are encrypted at rest (see “Security”).

Security

  • In transit: release builds of the app connect to the deployment over TLS. Every request after sign-in carries your session token. Every request is checked against your organisation’s tenant, so FACE refuses to serve one organisation’s records to another.
  • At rest: accounts, sessions, connection credentials and FACE’s application tables are written through an encrypted store, using envelope encryption (AES-GCM) under a key derived from the platform’s key. Retained evidence images are sealed the same way. Two limits we state rather than imply otherwise: storage file names are not encrypted, so anyone who can list the storage learns the organisation, date and camera label of a retained image without opening it. The spoken-reply audio cache is not encrypted.
  • Two-factor sign-in (an authenticator app) is available for password accounts, and an instance can require it.

No system is perfectly secure, and we do not claim FACE is.

How long we keep it

  • Sessions: 12 hours, or until you sign out.
  • Live-detection frames: not kept.
  • Sightings (grading results): FACE reads back 30 days. Older records are not deleted automatically.
  • Annotated evidence images: not kept unless the instance’s operator turns them on. When on, they are served for at most 30 days and then stay in storage until the operator expires them.
  • Your account record: kept until an administrator removes you, which deletes it.
  • Everything else FACE keeps for your organisation (questions, run history, attachment readings) has no automatic expiry today.

Deleting your account and your data

The app cannot delete an account by itself. The Profile page’s “Close account & take my data” button opens a request: it emails privacy@runink.org for you and links the account-deletion page. Nothing is deleted from that screen.

  • Your organisation’s FACE administrator can remove you from the instance on the Admin page. That deletes your FACE account record and ends your sessions.
  • To ask for your account and the personal data tied to it to be deleted, or to ask what is stored about you, email privacy@runink.org from the address you sign in with. Data that belongs to your organisation, rather than to you personally, is deleted at your organisation’s request.

Your choices

  • Camera: optional. Only the camera screen uses it.
  • Grading: nothing you photograph is sent for grading until you press Grade.
  • Microphone: optional and used only when you tap it. You can type instead.
  • Signing out ends your sessions on the server and removes your token from the phone.
  • Access, correction, export and deletion: email privacy@runink.org.

Children

FACE is a business console. It is not directed at children, and only people an organisation’s administrator has added can use it.

Changes

We will update this page when FACE’s behaviour changes, and update the date below.

Your rights and complaints

Runink is published by Technologies Runink Inc., Montréal, Québec, Canada.

You can ask us to show you the personal information we hold about you, to correct it, to delete it, or to give you a copy. Email privacy@runink.org. We read that mailbox, and we answer within 30 days.

If you are not satisfied with our answer, you can complain to the Commission d’accès à l’information du Québec or to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada.
